The moments Ana keeps on cue

Every wedding has its own order. These are the moments where a coordinator makes the biggest difference.

Ceremony and processional

Ana confirms timing with your church or officiant, lines up the wedding party and padrinos in order, and makes sure the photographer is in place before the first walk down the aisle.

Lazo, arras and padrinos

For a Mexican or Catholic wedding, she checks that each padrino has the lazo, arras, Bible or rosary and knows exactly when to step forward.

Grand entrance and first dance

The DJ has the right songs and names, the wedding party is ready, and the two of you walk in on time.

Toasts, cake and family dances

Speakers know their turn, the cake knife is where it should be, and the parent dances happen before guests start drifting.

Money dance and la víbora de la mar

If you include them, Ana starts and closes them on schedule and makes sure the money goes to the person you chose.

Send-off and wrap-up

She gathers your gifts and personal décor and confirms vendor breakdown, so you leave with nothing left to do.

Wedding coordination questions

What does a day-of wedding coordinator do?

A day-of wedding coordinator learns your plan in the months before the wedding, builds a detailed timeline, confirms every vendor, and then runs the wedding day itself: vendor setup, the ceremony lineup, the reception timeline and wrap-up. You make the decisions; the coordinator carries them out.

How much does a day-of wedding coordinator cost?

Ana quotes every wedding individually, because the cost depends on your date, your venue, how many hours the day needs (up to 6 on site) and how much planning support you want beforehand. Send your date and venue and she’ll get back to you with a quote.

When should we book a day-of coordinator?

As soon as you have your date and venue. Ana works with you in the months before the wedding, so booking early gives her time to meet your vendors and build the timeline with you.

What’s the difference between a wedding planner and a day-of coordinator?

A full wedding planner usually finds and books vendors for you. With a day-of coordinator, you choose and book your own vendors; Ana helps with recommendations and design, then takes over vendor communication, builds the timeline and runs the day.

Do you coordinate bilingual and Mexican weddings?

Yes. Ana works in English and Spanish and knows the traditions many Mexican and Mexican-American weddings include, from the lazo and arras to the money dance and la víbora de la mar.

Where do you coordinate weddings?

At venues and churches throughout Dallas–Fort Worth.
