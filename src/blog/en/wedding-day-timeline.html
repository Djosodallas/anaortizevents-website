A wedding day moves fast. Getting ready, the ceremony, photos, cocktail hour, and a reception full of moments that need to happen in the right order. A clear timeline is what keeps all of it connected, and it's what lets you and your guests relax and enjoy the day.

Here's how to think through each part of the day, followed by a sample schedule you can use as a starting point.

Start with your fixed times

Some times aren't up to you. Write those down first and build around them.

  • The ceremony. A church will set the time, often around its regular Mass schedule. Other ceremony sites may have their own windows.
  • Venue access. When can vendors start setting up, and when does everyone need to be out?
  • Vendor hours. Photographers, videographers, and DJs are usually booked for a set number of hours. Know when coverage starts and ends.
  • Sunset. If you want portraits in soft evening light, check the time for your date.

The parts of the day, in order

Getting ready

Hair and makeup almost always run longer than planned. Schedule the bride last so her look is fresh, and leave a buffer before she needs to be dressed. If your photographer is covering getting-ready photos, have the dress, rings, invitation, and other details set out for them.

First look (optional)

Some couples see each other privately before the ceremony so they can take portraits earlier and join more of cocktail hour. Others prefer to wait for the aisle. Either is fine. Just decide early, because it changes the whole photo schedule.

The ceremony

Plan for the wedding party to arrive 30 minutes or more before the start time. A Catholic wedding Mass often runs about an hour, while a non-Mass ceremony can be much shorter. Ask your church or officiant. If you're including the lazo, arras, or other traditions, make sure the padrinos know when they'll be called up.

Photos

Right after the ceremony is the easiest time to gather family for group photos, since everyone is already there. Keep the list of groupings short and assign someone who knows the family to round people up.

Cocktail hour

Guests arrive at the reception, find their seats, and enjoy drinks and appetizers while you finish photos. This is also the venue team's last chance to finish details, so it helps to have someone checking that the room is ready.

Grand entrance

The wedding party and padrinos are introduced, followed by the couple. Give your DJ or MC the correct spellings and pronunciations ahead of time, and confirm the order.

First dance

Many couples go straight from the entrance into the first dance while everyone's attention is on them. Parent dances can follow immediately or come later in the evening.

Dinner and toasts

Toasts often happen just before or during dinner, while guests are seated and listening. Let each speaker know how long they have, and tell your DJ who's speaking and in what order.

Cake cutting

Schedule the cake cutting after dinner and before the dance floor gets busy, so it's easy to gather guests and your photographer.

Optional traditions

These depend entirely on you and your families. Some couples include all of them, some include none.

  • Money dance: Guests pin money or offer a gift in exchange for a dance with the couple.
  • La víbora de la mar: A high-energy group dance that guests usually love. Plan for it while energy is high.
  • Bouquet and garter toss: Usually later in the evening, when the dance floor is full.

Whatever you choose, give your DJ the order ahead of time so these moments land on cue.

Open dancing and the send-off

Once the formal moments are done, the party takes over. Plan a last dance and a send-off, whether that's sparklers, bubbles, or a final song with everyone on the dance floor. Check your venue's rules first.

A sample wedding day timeline

This is an example to adapt, not a fixed schedule. Your ceremony time, venue rules, travel distance, and traditions will change it.

  • 9:30 a.m. Hair and makeup begins for the wedding party
  • 11:30 a.m. Hair and makeup for the bride
  • 1:00 p.m. Getting-ready photos and getting dressed
  • 1:45 p.m. Leave for the ceremony
  • 2:15 p.m. Wedding party arrives at the church
  • 3:00 p.m. Ceremony begins
  • 4:00 p.m. Family and wedding party photos
  • 2:00–5:30 p.m. Vendors arrive and set up at the reception venue
  • 5:30 p.m. Cocktail hour begins at the reception
  • 6:30 p.m. Grand entrance
  • 6:40 p.m. First dance
  • 6:50 p.m. Welcome and toasts
  • 7:00 p.m. Dinner is served
  • 8:00 p.m. Parent dances and cake cutting
  • 8:20 p.m. Open dancing
  • 9:30 p.m. Money dance and víbora de la mar (optional)
  • 10:15 p.m. Bouquet and garter toss (optional)
  • 11:45 p.m. Last dance and send-off
  • 12:00 a.m. Event ends, breakdown begins

Tips for a timeline that actually works

  • Add buffer between big moments. Ten minutes here and there absorbs late arrivals and long photo lists.
  • Write down who does what. Next to each item, note who's responsible: the DJ, the photographer, the padrinos, the wedding party.
  • Share it with every vendor. Send the final version about two weeks ahead, along with a day-of contact who isn't you.
  • Give the wedding party a short version. They need arrival times, locations, and their cues, not every detail.
  • Talk to your DJ about cues. A DJ who MCs in English and Spanish can keep both sides of the family involved. If you're still looking, DJ Oso is on our preferred vendors list.
