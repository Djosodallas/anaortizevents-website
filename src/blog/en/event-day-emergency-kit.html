On a wedding or quince day, small problems show up at the worst times. A zipper catches right before the bride walks down the aisle. A chambelán gets a blister halfway through the vals. Someone's phone dies just when you need to call the limo. None of these are disasters if the fix is already packed.

An emergency kit is one of the simplest things you can prepare, and it saves a lot of running around. Here is what to put in it, who should carry it, and a few extras for Texas weather.

Wardrobe fixes

The bride's or quinceañera's gown, the bridesmaids' or damas' dresses, and the groomsmen's or chambelanes' suits all get a workout on this day. Pack:

  • Small sewing kit with thread in white, black, and the colors of the gown and court outfits
  • Safety pins in several sizes, including some large ones for a gown bustle
  • Double-sided fashion tape for straps, necklines, and hems
  • Stain remover pen or wipes, plus a white cloth and a small bottle of water
  • Lint roller for dark suits and tuxedos
  • Extra buttons and a few clear elastic bands
  • Clear nail polish to stop a run in tights
  • Small scissors for loose threads and tags

Hair, makeup, and freshening up

  • Bobby pins in the bride's or quinceañera's hair color
  • Travel-size hairspray and a small comb
  • Blotting papers and pressed powder
  • Her lipstick or lip gloss, so it can be touched up for photos
  • Deodorant, preferably a clear or no-white-marks formula
  • Breath mints
  • Tissues and cotton swabs
  • Hand sanitizer
  • Feminine hygiene products

First aid and comfort

  • Bandages in several sizes, plus blister pads
  • Over-the-counter pain reliever
  • Antacid tablets
  • Allergy medicine
  • Eye drops
  • Any personal medications the couple, the quinceañera, or the parents need that day

Keep medications in their original labeled containers, and make sure only adults hand them out.

Comfortable shoes for the bride or quinceañera

This one deserves its own line. Between the ceremony, photos, the first dance or vals, and hours of dancing, her feet will be tired. Pack a pair of comfortable flats or sneakers that match her look or at least her color. Many brides change into them once the formal dances are done, and many quinceañeras after the baile sorpresa. Add a pair of clean socks too.

Put a few extra pairs of flip-flops or flats in a bag for the bridesmaids or damas as well. They will thank you.

Tech and tools

  • Phone chargers with both common cable types, plus a portable battery
  • Extension cord and a power strip
  • Clear tape and painter's tape for signs and loose décor
  • Lighter for candles, if the venue allows them
  • Zip ties and a few binder clips
  • Pen and marker
  • Printed copy of the timeline and the vendor contact list

Having the vendor list on paper matters more than people expect. If a phone dies, you still need to reach the DJ or the limo driver. Our guide to building your wedding day timeline is a good starting point, and the same approach works for a quince.

Snacks and water

Couples, quinceañeras, and their wedding party or court often skip lunch with everything going on, then dance for hours. Pack:

  • Bottled water, more than you think you need
  • Easy snacks that will not stain: crackers, pretzels, granola bars, fruit snacks
  • Straws, so she can drink without smudging her lipstick
  • Napkins

Texas heat backups

Dallas–Fort Worth heat can be intense for much of the year, and a full gown plus outdoor photos is a real challenge. Add these if any part of your day is outside or if you are traveling between the church and the salón in warm months:

  • Handheld or battery-powered fans
  • A cooler with water and a few cold towels
  • Sunscreen for arms and shoulders, applied before makeup is finished
  • An umbrella for shade during outdoor photos
  • Electrolyte drinks or packets
  • Extra deodorant and blotting papers

Keep the cars running with the air on before the court gets in, and plan outdoor photos for the cooler part of the day if you can.

Rain backups

Storms in DFW can come up quickly. If there is a chance of rain:

  • Pack a few large clear umbrellas. They look better in photos than dark ones.
  • Bring a towel to dry shoes and a hem after walking from the car.
  • Keep a large plastic bag or garment cover to protect the bottom of the gown.
  • Know where the covered entrances are at the church and the venue.
  • Confirm the indoor backup plan with your venue ahead of time.

Who should hold the kit

The kit only works if someone has it when it is needed. A few good rules:

  • Do not give it to the bride, the groom, or the quinceañera's mother. They need to be in photos, greeting guests, and enjoying the day.
  • Choose one responsible person, like an aunt, a trusted friend, or your coordinator.
  • Split it into two bags if the ceremony and reception are in different places: a small one for church and a full one at the venue.
  • Tell the wedding party or the court where it is so they know who to find for a broken strap or a blister.

Pack it the week before

Do not leave the kit for the morning of the event. Put it together a few days ahead, check it against this list, and set it by the door with the dress, the shoes, and the tradition items. That way, the morning of the wedding or quince is about getting ready and enjoying it, not looking for safety pins.
