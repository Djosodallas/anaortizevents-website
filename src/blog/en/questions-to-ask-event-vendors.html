Booking vendors is one of the biggest steps in planning a wedding or a quinceañera. A friendly conversation and a nice portfolio are a good start, but the details that matter most on the day are usually buried in the contract and the logistics.

These are the questions I'd want answered before signing with anyone. Bring them to your calls, take notes, and compare answers side by side.

Questions for every vendor

Start with these no matter who you're talking to.

  • Are you available on my date, and how do I hold it? Ask what deposit is required and whether it's refundable.
  • What exactly is included? Get the specifics in writing: hours, items, people, delivery.
  • Who will be at my event? The owner, or someone from their team? Can you meet them beforehand?
  • What time will you arrive, and how long does setup take? This needs to fit the venue's access window.
  • What happens if the event runs long? Ask how overtime is charged and who has to approve it.
  • What's your backup plan? If someone gets sick or equipment fails, what happens?
  • Are you insured? Many venues require vendors to carry liability insurance.
  • What's your payment schedule and cancellation policy? Know every due date and what happens if the date changes.
  • Have you worked at my venue before? Not required, but familiarity with load-in, parking, and power helps.

Questions to ask a wedding venue

  • How many guests can the space hold for a seated dinner with a dance floor?
  • What are the access hours for setup and the hard end time for breakdown?
  • Which tables, chairs, and linens are included?
  • Do you require us to use specific vendors, or can we bring our own caterer and decorator?
  • Are there rules about candles, confetti, fog machines, or wall decorations?
  • Is there a kitchen or prep area for the caterer?
  • How much parking is there, and is security required?

Questions to ask a wedding DJ or MC

  • Do you MC as well as play music? In both English and Spanish?
  • Have you done weddings or quinceañeras like ours, and are you comfortable with the order of traditions (first dance, money dance, vals, surprise dance)?
  • Can we give you a must-play and do-not-play list?
  • Do you provide lighting, a microphone for the toast, or extra speakers for a separate area?
  • How do you handle cues for the grand entrance, first dance or vals, toasts, and other key moments?
  • What equipment do you bring as backup?

Questions to ask a wedding photographer and videographer

  • How many hours of coverage are included, and when does it start?
  • Will you cover the ceremony or Misa? Do you know the church's rules on flash and movement?
  • Can I see a full gallery from one event, not just highlights?
  • When will we receive photos and video, and in what format?
  • Do you bring a second shooter or assistant?
  • Can we give you a list of must-have shots and family groupings?

Caterer

  • Is a tasting available before we book or before final menu selection?
  • Is service buffet, plated, or family style, and how many servers will be there?
  • Are plates, utensils, and serving equipment included?
  • When do you need the final guest count?
  • Can you accommodate dietary restrictions or a kids' menu?
  • Do you clean up and take away leftovers or trash?
  • Do you have the permits or insurance the venue requires?

Decorator or florist

  • Can we see photos of setups similar to what we want?
  • What is included: centerpieces, backdrop, head table, entrance décor?
  • Do you handle setup and breakdown, and how long does each take?
  • Are any items rentals that need to be returned the same night?
  • Will you coordinate with the venue on what's allowed?
  • Can our personal décor (family photos, signs, the lazo or the last doll) be worked into the design?

Cake and desserts

  • Do you deliver and set up, and at what time?
  • Is a stand or display included?
  • How do you handle hot weather or a long setup before the reception?
  • Will you cut and serve, or does someone else need to?

Hair, makeup, and others

For hair and makeup, ask whether they come to you, how long each person takes, and whether a trial is included. For transportation, confirm the pickup addresses, wait time, and what happens if the ceremony runs late. For rentals like photo booths or dance floors, ask about delivery and pickup windows.

Reading the contract

Before you sign, read every page. A few things worth checking:

  • The date, venue address, and your name are correct.
  • Arrival time, setup time, and service hours are written out.
  • The total, deposit, and every payment due date are listed.
  • The overtime rate is stated.
  • The cancellation and rescheduling policy is clear.
  • What happens if the vendor can't make it.

If something was promised in conversation and isn't in the contract, ask for it to be added.
