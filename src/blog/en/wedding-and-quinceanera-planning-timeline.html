Weddings and quinceañeras look different on the surface, but the planning follows a very similar path. There's a ceremony to arrange, a reception to design, a group of loved ones standing beside the guest of honor, and a list of vendors who all need to arrive at the right time. When you spread those decisions across the year, each month feels manageable.

This checklist starts about 12 months out and works for both events. Where the steps differ, you'll see a note for each. If you have less time, start at the top, book the big pieces first, and keep moving.

12 months out: set the foundation

  • Pick a date and a backup. If you're having a church ceremony or a Misa, check with your parish before anything else.
  • Set a budget. Decide what you can comfortably spend and who is contributing. Leave some room for the small costs that add up near the end.
  • Estimate the guest count. It doesn't need to be final, but it decides which venues will work.
  • Talk about the vision. For a wedding, that conversation is between the couple. For a quince, ask your daughter what matters most to her: colors, theme, the feel of the night.

10 to 11 months out: venue and ceremony

Popular venues in Dallas–Fort Worth book early, especially Saturdays in spring and fall. Tour a few, make a list of must-haves (capacity, parking, kitchen access, end time), and read the contract carefully before you sign.

  • Book the reception venue and confirm what's included: tables, chairs, linens, setup and cleanup windows.
  • Reserve the ceremony location or church and ask about requirements. Many parishes ask for marriage preparation before a wedding, or classes and paperwork before a quinceañera Misa.
  • For a wedding, book your officiant if you're not marrying in a church.
  • Start thinking about padrinos and ask them early so they have time to plan.

8 to 9 months out: key vendors

Once the date and venue are locked in, book the vendors who can only be at one event per day.

  • Photographer and videographer
  • DJ or band (ideally one who can MC in English and Spanish if your guests are bilingual)
  • Caterer, if the venue doesn't provide food
  • Decorator or florist
  • Day-of coordinator, if you want one

Go into each call with a list of questions to ask event vendors so you can compare them fairly.

6 to 7 months out: attire and your people

  • Wedding: Shop for the wedding dress and plan for alterations. Choose your wedding party and decide what they'll wear.
  • Quinceañera: Shop for the quince dress. Choose the damas, chambelanes, and chambelán de honor, and find a choreographer for the vals and surprise dance. Ask early so court families can plan for rehearsals and attire.
  • Both: Book the cake vendor and send save-the-dates if you're using them.

4 to 5 months out: invitations and details

  • Finalize the guest list and gather addresses.
  • Order invitations.
  • Book hair and makeup and schedule a trial.
  • Arrange transportation if needed.
  • Wedding: Order the items for traditions you plan to include, such as the lazo, arras, Bible, and rosary, and confirm the padrinos who will present them.
  • Quinceañera: Start court rehearsals and order traditional items like the last doll, tiara, and shoes for the changing of the shoes.

3 months out: send invitations and plan the music

  • Mail invitations about eight to ten weeks before the date, with a clear RSVP deadline.
  • Choose music for the key moments and share it with your DJ: processional, entrance, first dance or vals, surprise dance, last dance.
  • Walk the venue so you can picture where everything goes.
  • For a wedding, look into your marriage license requirements and timing so it's ready for the ceremony.

2 months out: confirm and refine

  • Check in with every vendor to confirm the date, address, and what they're bringing.
  • Schedule a menu tasting if your caterer offers one.
  • Decide on favors, the guest book, and personal décor like family photos or a welcome sign.
  • Go to the next dress fitting.

1 month out: lock in the details

  • Follow up on missing RSVPs and give the final count to the venue and caterer.
  • Make the seating chart.
  • Build the day-of timeline, from getting ready to the send-off. Couples can start with our wedding day timeline.
  • Make final payments according to each contract.
  • Confirm the ceremony details: readings, who reads them, and any rehearsal the church or officiant requires.

2 weeks out: share the plan

  • Send the final timeline to every vendor, with arrival times and a day-of contact.
  • Give the wedding party or court and their families their schedule: when to arrive, what to wear, where to be.
  • Pick up the dress and do a final try-on with shoes and accessories.
  • Pack personal décor and label each box with where it goes.

The week of: rest and delegate

  • Hold the ceremony rehearsal (wedding) or the final dance rehearsal (quince).
  • Confirm arrival times with each vendor once more.
  • Pack a bag with the ceremony items, the dress, and a small emergency kit: safety pins, a sewing kit, pain reliever, snacks, phone chargers.
  • Decide who handles questions on the day so it isn't you.
  • Get some sleep.
